What these experiments show — and their limits

These are deterministic educational illustrations, not AI models, measured trials or food-safety tools. The first experiment sums temperature above an arbitrary 4°C baseline over time; it does not estimate freshness, shelf life or safety. The second uses a fixed triangular spike and samples starting at minute zero, so results depend on timing. The third is arithmetic: the selected percentage is an absolute share of throughput, not an observed or promised reduction. Its crates illustrate magnitude, not individual shipments.

Appropriate conditions depend on the food and process. The UNEP–FAO sustainable cold chains report explains the broader opportunity. These experiments illustrate why context and evidence matter before applying AI.
